## Question
Could some functional seizures be state-transition failures in a dynamically compensated neural/autonomic control system, potentially involving differential energetic / substrate demand when distinct catecholamine-mediated circuits become more or less prominent?

Do **not** smuggle into the premises that the brain universally operates in one dopamine-dominant versus noradrenaline-dominant mode. Dopamine and noradrenaline have overlapping, interacting, region-specific functions; noradrenaline is synthesised from dopamine in noradrenergic neurons. “Functional dominance” is a tentative, operational systems abstraction, NOT mutually exclusive transmitter supply.

## Competing models (state them before evaluating)

- **H1: Resource-coupled circuit switching.** A particular neural/motor/autonomic control regime makes distinct metabolic, ionic, vesicular, synthesis or feedback demands; transition to it can expose an insufficiently replenished or poorly cleared resource and trigger a rapid loss of control.
- **H2: Stress/autonomic hyperarousal without substrate shortage.** Attacks correlate with arousal and dysregulated sympathetic/vagal signalling without requiring depletion of a unique dopamine-coupled resource.
- **H3: Network switching / prediction/attention/inhibitory control.** Episodes occur through altered functional coupling, attention or network-state shifts, without substrate exhaustion being causal.
- **H4: General physiological load/circadian disruption.** Sleep, heat, orthostasis, nutrition, medications, inflammation or global fatigue affect all networks rather than a specialised resource.
- **H5: Another medical event.** Epilepsy, arrhythmia, hypertensive crisis, migraine, syncope, endocrine disturbance and combinations may produce similar symptoms. Never assume a dangerous measured physiological event is functional just because FND appears on a record.

## Frozen predictions — each with direction, discriminant, and failure case
The below are **predictions**, not findings. Study ethically in naturally occurring clinically assessed episodes; no deliberate seizures, illicit-drug titrations, medication withholding or provocative challenges.

| ID | IF H1 (resource-coupled switch) | THEN predicted measurement | What would weaken H1? | Main comparators / pitfalls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P01 | A relatively specific resource/maintenance variable approaches a boundary | A within-person change in objectively measurable reserve or its validated proxy **precedes** onset, rather than appearing only after; trajectory should be nonlinear near onset | No predictive change in candidate variable after sufficiently powered repeated measurement; change starts only after attack | H2/H3 may also have pre-event autonomic/nonlinear patterns; biomarkers need validated CNS relevance |
| P02 | Different neural states rely on measurably different resource demand | The pre-event resource trend interacts with independently measured task/network state; **same general arousal** but different state yields distinct attack risk | Equal risk explained wholly by arousal/sleep or no state × reserve interaction | Brain states must be defined independently of outcome, not “normal until seizure” |
| P03 | Attacks are triggered by crossing a dynamic availability threshold | Risk sharply rises when a *pre-specified* within-person marker passes a reproducible boundary, with individual-specific lag | Threshold moves freely post hoc, or associations disappear on out-of-sample data | Hysteresis/nonlinear switching can arise in H2/H3 too |
| P04 | A depleted variable constrains recovery | Recovery time co-varies with rate of restoration of a validated resource or clearance marker, after adjusting for attack duration, autonomic load, sleep and medication | Functional recovery uncoupled from proposed variable or fully explained by sleep/medication | Avoid using post-event fatigue as a circular ‘resource depleted’ definition |
| P05 | The post-episode period has multiple components | Different recovery variables recover with distinct time constants (e.g. cardiac activation, orientation, motor control, pain/fatigue) | A single common recovery factor fully accounts for all domains across repeated episodes | Multiple time constants are not unique to H1; H2/H3 can also predict them |
| P06 | The proposed vulnerability is not just 'high norepinephrine' | Directly relevant catecholamine-linked markers show a state-specific dissociation not fully predicted by HR/HRV or threat/arousal measures | Same marker profile in panic, physiological exertion, and FDS when matched for arousal | Peripheral blood or saliva catecholamines do NOT map directly to brain synaptic concentration |
| P07 | A particular bottleneck causes selective functional loss | Tasks reliant on the hypothesised circuit degrade before more independent tasks, matched for effort and attentional demand | Nonselective performance collapse; observed selection disappears after blinded task analysis | “Dopamine task” labels must be neuroscientifically defensible; motor output and cognition are multi-transmitter |
| P08 | Preceding chronic compensation matters | History of repeated lower-grade autonomic/motor compensations predicts FDS onset/recovery beyond ordinary anxiety, sleep loss and comorbidity | No incremental predictive value once confounders entered | Risk of selecting only complex patients and tautologically relabeling fatigue |
| P09 | Abnormal clearance, not depletion, is the limiting variable | Markers of candidate accumulation/clearance lag change before the event and predict delayed recovery, with **different directional signatures** from depletion model | No compatible marker kinetics; predictions fitted only after examining assay results | Cytokines/oxidative markers nonspecific; blood samples can reflect stress/illness |
| P10 | Dopamine downregulation is an *adaptive compensation* for toxic resource load | In a pre-defined subgroup, reduced candidate-network activity precedes reduced event rate under equal global stress, but worsens selected function; marker of toxicity/resource imbalance drives trade-off | Global improvement/reduction in all function alongside lower event rate cannot be distinguished; no measurable resource/toxicity signature | Very high risk of reverse causation and medication confounding; do not assume adaptive intent from correlation |
| P11 | H1 has clinically important specificity | Predictive model transfers *within the same mechanism subgroup* across attacks, yet differs in calibrated ways from matched epilepsy, panic, syncope and high-autonomic-arousal controls | Model predicts all episodic conditions equally or fails prospective validation | Misclassified seizures/coexisting epilepsy, selection biases, different testing conditions |

## Ordering for **non-biased evidence assessment**
1. Freeze this version of the H1–H5 assumptions, P01–P11, variables and negative conditions.
2. Identify the strongest **basic biochemical plausibility constraints** for any proposed candidate resource: dopamine/noradrenaline synthesis compartmentalisation (tyrosine, TH and cofactors; DBH and vesicles), vesicular proton gradient, ATP production and utilisation, membrane excitability, receptor/transport dynamics, cerebral perfusion and neurovascular coupling. Do not mistake “biologically real” for “abnormal in FDS.”
3. Seek opposing observations / negative controls *as deliberately as supportive observations*.
4. Construct evidence matrix by P-ID: direct FDS evidence | indirect related-condition evidence | contradictory findings | bias/limitations | expected signs/timing | status.
5. Rank only afterwards based on specificity, reproducibility, feasibility of measuring the implicated variable and strength of direct evidence; allow an explicit **indeterminate** result.
6. Refine or retire H1 sub-claims explicitly, never silently rewrite this frozen v0.1 to match data. Create a subsequent v0.2 with change log.

## Measurement priorities (clinical setting; observational only)
- Confirm event diagnosis and coexisting epilepsy where relevant using specialist review and recorded representative events.
- Baseline/prodrome/episode/early recovery/late recovery: event timestamp and phenotype, validated ECG rhythm/HRV, properly taken BP, respiration, sleep/activity/medications, perceived warning signs, fatigue, pain, cognitive/motor testing where appropriate.
- Candidate biomarkers only if mechanism-specific and their peripheral-to-central inference limitations are explicit; use matched timing, repeated patient-level sampling, safe collection and ethics oversight.
- Control groups: functional seizures with and without comorbid epilepsy; epileptic events; panic/dysautonomia; exercise/heat; non-event matched periods within the same person.
- Patient and family participation in symptom selection; record post-episode costs rather than just event frequency.
- Safety: do not use a pressure reading such as 253/159 with pulse 155 as evidence for a functional cause without urgent medical assessment. Whether it lasted >24h requires original serial observations; anecdote is a pointer, not verification.

## Pre-evidence status
These are falsifiable *ideas*. Some predictions (P01, P03, P05) overlap with generic dynamical-system theories and would be weakly discriminating alone. Candidate-resource specificity (P02, P06, P07, P09, P11) is necessary for meaningful causal inference. This register was written after the conversation had already included an initial literature discussion and is therefore not a blinded preregistration. Future claims must reference actual studies separately.
