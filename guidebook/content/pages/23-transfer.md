@layer 1 Quick understanding
Three handovers commonly break: **specialist → GP** (shared care), **one specialist → another** (including Right to Choose providers), and **service → nobody** (discharge without a receiving prescriber). In each case the safe default is the same: the current prescriber stays responsible until the next one has accepted.

!institution(P5) Ministers have said GP practices may decline ADHD shared care, and that responsibility then stays with the specialist.

@layer 2 Possible explanations
- GP practices may decline shared care for capacity or confidence reasons, especially with independent providers.
- Local commissioning policies change, and old rules can be quoted after they have been replaced.

!institution(P3) In Coventry and Warwickshire, the commissioner's 2025 ADHD referral policy (COM/46) stated that patients already on the waiting list or under follow-up would be funded; project records indicate it was replaced by an all-age policy from 14 May 2026, whose text should be obtained before relying on either.

@layer 3 Useful next action
Ask any new provider, in writing:
- [ ] Will you prescribe directly until a shared-care agreement is accepted?
- [ ] What records do you need from the previous service, and will you request them?
- [ ] What is your restart plan and review schedule?
- [ ] Who do I contact between appointments?

@uncertainty
Commissioning rules vary by area and change over time. Check the operative version and its date.
@unresolved
The replacement all-age Coventry and Warwickshire policy (from 14 May 2026) has not yet been obtained.
