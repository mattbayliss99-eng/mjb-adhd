@layer 1 Quick understanding
When a coroner believes action could prevent future deaths, they must write a Prevention of Future Deaths report, and the recipient must reply within 56 days. These reports are public. They are written about one person's death, with care, and are summarised here with the same care.

Only cases checked against the coroner's own report appear here. Others remain in the research queue until checked.

!finding(COR-2024-0699-WINSON) Oliver Winson died aged 33 after four years on an adult ADHD waiting list. The Norfolk coroner's report to NHS England raised concern that people identified as at risk from untreated ADHD remain on long waiting lists without treatment or monitoring, while evidence also noted a medication shortage for those already diagnosed.

@layer 5 Deeper explanation
A report records concerns, not a finding that a particular failure caused a death. These reports show that the risks discussed elsewhere in this guide have been recognised by coroners — they do not provide rates.

@uncertainty
Coroners' reports cover only deaths that reach inquest and in which the coroner chose to report; they are not a count.
@unresolved
Other cases cited in earlier campaign material are awaiting verification against the published reports and responses.
