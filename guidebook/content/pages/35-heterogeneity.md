@layer 1 Quick understanding
The simple version — "ADHD is low dopamine" — is too simple. Some studies find lower dopamine markers, some find higher, and some differences appear to be adaptations to treatment rather than causes.

!research[moderate](R5) In 53 unmedicated adults, dopamine transporter and D2/D3 receptor binding were lower in reward-pathway regions than in controls, and lower binding correlated with inattention.

!contested(R6) Across nine imaging studies, transporter density was on average 14% higher in ADHD, but strongly dependent on prior stimulant exposure: lower in drug-naive people and higher in previously medicated people.

@layer 2 Possible explanations
- **Several routes**: different combinations of genetic and developmental differences can produce similar day-to-day difficulties.
- **Adaptation**: the system re-tunes after long-term treatment, changing what a scan shows.
- **Region matters**: prefrontal cortex and striatum regulate dopamine differently.
- **Co-occurring conditions** add their own signals.

@layer 5 Deeper explanation
Heterogeneity is not a weakness of the ADHD concept. It is what you expect from a polygenic, developmental condition — and it predicts that people will differ in which medicine, dose and formulation suits them, which is what clinical experience shows.

!research[strong](W5) Most ADHD-influencing genetic variants are shared with other psychiatric conditions, supporting overlapping mechanisms rather than a single dedicated pathway.

@uncertainty
There are no validated subtypes that predict which medicine an individual will respond to.
