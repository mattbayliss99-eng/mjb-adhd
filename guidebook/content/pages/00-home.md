@layer 1 The idea behind this guide
### Simple parts, complicated control
The machinery that ADHD medicines act on is made of a small number of parts: an enzyme that makes dopamine, a pump that stores it, a release step, transporters that clear it, enzymes that break it down, and receptors that read it. That short list is the same in every textbook. What varies between people — and what makes ADHD hard to pin down — is how those parts are built, tuned and coordinated across development and across a day.

This guide is organised the same way: a few clear entrances at the top, then progressively finer detail, until each route ends at a decision, a question or a piece of evidence you can check. Start with [[adhd-biology|how ADHD works]], or with [[continuity|what to do when treatment stops]].

> [!note] What this guide will not do
> It will not diagnose anyone, tell anyone to change a dose, or present a hypothesis as a finding. Where the evidence runs out, the page says so and turns the gap into a question.

@uncertainty
The guide combines sourced explanations, documented accounts and open research questions. It is not an exhaustive assessment of every cause or treatment.
