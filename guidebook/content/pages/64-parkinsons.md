@layer 1 Here is the condition
!guidance(F10) Parkinson disease involves loss of dopamine-producing cells in the substantia nigra, affecting movement control. This important feature is a simplified explanation of a wider disorder; parkinsonism also has other causes.

@layer 2 How it is recognised
!guidance(F11) Diagnosis depends on the symptom history and a detailed clinical examination; there is no single conclusive routine test. A suspected pattern needs specialist assessment.

Pain quality, a feeling of compression or an improvement after a stimulant cannot by itself establish Parkinson disease. See [[nerve-pain]] and [[dystonia]] to describe those experiences separately.

@layer 3 Useful next action
Describe changes in walking, movement speed, stiffness and tremor, together with their timeline and effects on everyday tasks. Ask what findings support the suspected diagnosis, what alternatives remain, and which assessment will distinguish them.

@layer 4 What treatment targets
!guidance(F12) Levodopa is converted into dopamine and is commonly combined with a medicine limiting its breakdown outside the brain. Other options include dopamine agonists and enzyme inhibitors; therapies can address practical function. Benefits and adverse effects differ.

!guidance(F9) NICE recommends levodopa when early Parkinson motor symptoms affect quality of life. Dopaminergic treatments require discussion of impulse-control problems, with greater risk associated with dopamine agonists.

@layer 5 Where the standard answer stops
“Low dopamine” does not explain every symptom, establish the cause of cell loss or make every dopamine-related substance interchangeable. Symptom relief and slowing the underlying disease are different outcomes. An option should state which outcome its evidence supports.

Dopamine agonists directly target the dopamine system; calling them merely stabilisers misdescribes their mechanism. Their risks justify specific monitoring and comparison, rather than assuming they never help.
