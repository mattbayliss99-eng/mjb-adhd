# Neurological field guide: first research pass

The five added pages are `field-guide`, `dopamine-options`, `botanical-evidence`, `parkinsons-field-guide` and `case-decisions`. They are fully connected to existing condition/treatment/care routes.

## Editorial structure

1. Name the condition or syndrome.
2. Explain the main affected function and the limits of simplification.
3. Explain how the clinical pattern is recognised and distinguished.
4. Audit a specific familiar claim, with its source and date.
5. Compare treatment purposes, evidence, preparation, access and monitoring.
6. Finish with a useful next question and what remains unresolved.

Critique targets a stated claim or care decision. It cannot presume that every standard answer is wrong. The guide should challenge oversimplified mainstream explanations and overconfident alternative explanations with the same evidence test.

## Findings worth developing

- The saffron ADHD pilot is worth covering, but does not establish adult efficacy, selective DRI action, clinical equivalence or long-term comparative safety. No Mucuna-plus-saffron ADHD trial was reviewed.
- Mucuna deserves a real treatment-research entry. The newer study's small open-label design, characterised preparation and Parkinson population matter, as do the earlier trial's discontinuations and commercial-product variability.
- Broad complementary-treatment wording on an older public information page needs checking against dated, condition-specific primary trials. This is an example of an evidence update, not proof that all conventional treatment is discredited.
- Atomoxetine and guanfacine should be evaluated for ADHD outcomes and tolerability; guanfacine's adrenergic receptor target must not be confused with dopamine agonism. Dopamine agonists are actual Parkinson treatments with material risks.
- Cocaine, crack and methamphetamine can be described accurately without presenting an illicit self-treatment ladder. Mechanism overlap is insufficient to establish substitutability.
- The case page distinguishes the trust's account, the patient's dispute and unanswered ownership/assessment questions. Private originals and their identifiers remain outside the public package.

## Next research queue

Condition review: FND, neuropathy subtypes/small-fibre neuropathy, multiple sclerosis, migraine, autonomic dysfunction, epilepsy and sleep disorders. Each needs its own primary/institutional evidence pass; do not fill these with generic dopamine explanations.

Botanical review: search all relevant saffron RCTs and registrations, distinguish monotherapy/adjunct trials, ages, blinding, product standardisation and longer-term outcomes. Check completed and negative trials. Reassess Mucuna trials by stage of Parkinson disease, formulation and decarboxylase-inhibitor context.

Access review: verify current UK licensing and specialist pathways by product before labelling any medicine widely available. US labels identify a product/indication, not UK access.

Mechanism review: separate human clinical outcomes from in-vitro, animal and proposed mechanisms. Add quantitative comparative diagrams only after identifying valid comparable data.

This first pass contains targeted sources and explicit gaps. It does not warrant a global efficacy/safety ranking, a clinical recommendation for an individual, or a claim to cover the entire neurological space.
