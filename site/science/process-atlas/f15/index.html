GABA transmission and inhibition

GABA responses depend on receptor type and the ion gradient. GABA is usually inhibitory in mature contexts; that is not an absolute rule.

What the diagram shows

GABA is produced from glutamate and released from vesicles. GABAA receptors conduct anions, so the effect depends on chloride/bicarbonate gradients and membrane potential, not just the name of the transmitter. GABAB receptors act through G proteins. Developmental induction of KCC2 contributes to more hyperpolarising GABAA responses in studied neurons. A depolarising response and net network excitation are also not automatically identical. The diagram makes no individual ADHD or developmental diagnosis.
