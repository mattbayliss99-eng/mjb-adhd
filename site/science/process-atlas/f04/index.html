Vesicle loading and storage

ATP powers the vesicle's proton pump. VMAT2 uses the resulting proton gradient to load monoamines for storage and later release; it is not directly powered by ATP. This normal mechanism does not establish an individual transport defect.

What the diagram shows

ATP hydrolysis powers the vesicular proton pump, V-ATPase, which acidifies the vesicle. VMAT2 exchanges vesicular protons for cytosolic monoamines such as dopamine. It therefore uses an existing proton gradient rather than directly hydrolysing ATP itself. Stored transmitter can subsequently be released by exocytosis. This explains a normal mechanism, not evidence of a VMAT2 defect in a particular reader.
