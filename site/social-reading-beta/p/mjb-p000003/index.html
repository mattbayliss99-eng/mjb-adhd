main idea · Explanation 1 of 4

The short version

This is a map of how one dopamine-releasing nerve cell in the brain makes, stores and releases dopamine, and of what then happens to the dopamine.

The biology of each step is well established (Tier A). Whether a weak step explains ADHD in any particular person is an open question (Tier D), and the map does not answer it.

plain explanation · Explanation 2 of 4

Main line: inside a brain dopamine cell

Tyrosine in the blood is carried into the brain by LAT1.

Tyrosine hydroxylase needs BH4 to work.

Tyrosine hydroxylase's product, L-DOPA, is converted to dopamine by AADC.

Newly made dopamine is loaded into vesicles by VMAT2.

Filled vesicles release dopamine when calcium enters the nerve ending.

evidence context · Explanation 3 of 4

What genetic studies do and do not show here

None of the genes on this map is listed within 50 kb of any of the 27 regions reported by the 2023 genome-wide study (GEN-12).

An association with a region of DNA is not the same as a candidate gene, and neither is a proven causal mechanism (GEN-02).

practical context · Explanation 4 of 4

Where medicines act

Methylphenidate mainly inhibits the dopamine and noradrenaline transporters. Amphetamine also inhibits them, can make them run in reverse, and inhibits VMAT2.

They are not a guide to choosing or changing treatment, which is a decision for a prescriber.
