Science: process atlas

Figure-by-figure process atlas (interactive diagrams stay on the original pages).

Existing published pages, unchanged. "Read in shell" shows the original article text, sources and links inside this reader; downloads, interactive diagrams and the accessibility toolbar remain on the original page.

  • Whole system overview

    How do biology and context connect to cognitive function? This is an organising model, not one ADHD formula or a diagnostic graph.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f00/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Genes regulation and development

    A genetic association and a molecular mechanism answer different questions. A pathway gene is not automatically an ADHD risk gene.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f01/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Amino acid supply and brain entry

    Supply, transport and synthesis are separate control points. A blood level or dietary increase does not directly measure a synapse.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f02/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Catecholamine synthesis and cell type branches

    The catecholamine branch depends on which enzymes a cell expresses. Not every dopamine-producing neuron makes noradrenaline or adrenaline.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f03/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Vesicle loading and storage

    ATP powers the vesicle's proton pump. VMAT2 uses the resulting proton gradient to load monoamines for storage and later release; it is not directly powered by ATP. This normal mechanism does not establish an individual transport defect.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f04/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Release and vesicle recycling

    Electrical activity, calcium entry and vesicle fusion are distinct steps. Vesicle membrane recycling is different from amino-acid recycling.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f05/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Synaptic clearance and intracellular metabolism

    Transport out of the cleft and intracellular breakdown are different routes. MAO is not an enzyme floating freely in the synaptic cleft.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f06/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Receptors and intracellular response

    Receptor coupling is a starting point, not a complete prediction. A receptor family cannot be labelled universally good or bad for attention.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f07/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Energy supply and neuronal demand

    Neurons use energy to maintain gradients and support signalling. This is normal physiology, not proof of an energy defect in ADHD.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f08/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Dopamine and noradrenaline projection systems

    Selected projection examples: schematic routes, not anatomical positions. Projection systems are not one-to-one maps from a transmitter to a symptom.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f09/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Large scale brain networks

    Connectivity measures describe relationships in recorded brain activity. Dashed lines show statistical dependence, not axons or proven causal influence.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f10/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Cognitive functions and observable outcomes

    A cognitive profile includes several functions and several kinds of measurement. No single transmitter amount predicts the entire cognitive profile.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f11/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • BH4 synthesis and cofactor recycling

    Making BH4, recycling it after hydroxylases and redox salvage are distinct. The hydroxylase cofactor cycle is not identical to NOS redox salvage.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f12/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Vitamin and mineral support

    A required cofactor is not evidence that extra intake will help. Cofactor requirement does not establish deficiency or a supplement benefit.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f13/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Glutamate transmission

    Glutamate transmission combines vesicle loading, receptors and uptake. An excitatory transmitter pathway is not a measure of ADHD severity.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f14/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • GABA transmission and inhibition

    GABA responses depend on receptor type and the ion gradient. GABA is usually inhibitory in mature contexts; that is not an absolute rule.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f15/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Serotonin supply and transmission

    Brain and peripheral serotonin are separate compartments. Serotonin is not simply a happiness chemical; tissue context matters.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f16/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Acetylcholine and attention modulation

    Acetylcholine is made, released, hydrolysed and its choline reused. DAT and MAO are not the main acetylcholine clearance pathway.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f17/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Astrocytes and transmitter recycling

    Astrocytes support transmitter handling and the cellular environment. Amino-acid recycling is different from recycling a vesicle membrane.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f18/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Redox balance and glutathione

    Glutathione participates in redox chemistry; balance matters. This normal redox system does not establish oxidative stress as an ADHD cause.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f19/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • One carbon metabolism and methyl donors

    One-carbon transfer and SAM-dependent methylation are linked processes. A biochemical pathway is not a personalised supplement prescription.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f20/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Sleep circadian timing and arousal

    Circadian timing, sleep pressure and arousal interact but are not identical. Sleep loss can worsen performance without establishing the origin of ADHD.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f21/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Adrenal hormones: two tissue compartments

    Cortisol and adrenaline come from different adrenal tissues. Peripheral stress hormones cannot be read as a brain dopamine measurement.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f22/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Development synapse formation and maintenance

    Developmental biology and genetic association require different evidence. Rare-gene examples belong to the rare-variant source, not the 2023 GWAS.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f23/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Medication absorption metabolism and elimination

    Drug exposure is not the same thing as synaptic transmitter metabolism. These steps do not supply a dose or justify changing a prescribed regimen.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f24/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Medication target overlays

    Three medicines, different target actions: a simplified comparison. A target diagram is not a medicine ranking or a personal treatment choice.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f25/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Mitochondrial quality control: one mitophagy route

    One mitochondrial quality-control pathway: PINK1–PRKN mitophagy. A normal quality-control pathway does not establish an ADHD mechanism.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f26/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Immune signalling and neural context

    Immune signalling and neural context are research questions, not a universal cause. Established immune biology is not proof that ADHD is universally inflammatory.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f27/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Nutrition: supply, transport and evidence limits

    Nutrients pass through absorption and regulated transport before brain use. Gut transmitter production is not a direct measure of brain synaptic supply.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f28/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Blood flow oxygen and glucose delivery

    Oxygen and glucose have different delivery and membrane-entry routes. Reduced delivery and impaired cellular use are different hypotheses.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f29/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Histamine and orexin arousal modules

    Histamine and orexin are distinct systems with interacting arousal roles. Neither system supplies a one-to-one map to an ADHD symptom.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f30/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Contextual genetic overlay

    Interpret a genetic result through evidence, not a gene-name shortcut. Rare biochemical disorders and common ADHD association are not interchangeable.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f31/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • TH reaction and regulation

    Tyrosine hydroxylase chemistry: the reaction and its controls are separate. This is a cytosolic reaction; the arrow is not a direct route to a symptom.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f32/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • DBH and PNMT compartment detail

    DBH acts in secretory vesicles; PNMT acts in cytosol of expressing cells. These branches require the relevant enzymes; not every neuron has both.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f33/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • DAT uptake and ion gradients

    DAT transport uses ion gradients; ATP maintains those gradients indirectly. DAT is not directly powered by ATP hydrolysis.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f34/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • PFC receptor signalling in a defined context

    A specific experimental context: prefrontal working-memory circuits. More stimulation does not mean more benefit in every context.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f35/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Mitochondrial membrane and respiratory chain

    Electrons pass along the respiratory chain while complexes I, III and IV pump protons. Proton return through ATP synthase supports ATP formation. This illustrates general cell physiology; it does not establish an energy defect in a reader.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f36/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • BH4 hydroxylase recycling and salvage

    Hydroxylase recycling and oxidised-BH2 salvage require different enzymes. Quinonoid BH2 and 7,8-BH2 must not be treated as one identical intermediate.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f37/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Glutamate glutamine cellular shuttle

    The glutamate–glutamine shuttle links neurons and astrocytes. Astrocytic amino-acid recycling is not synaptic-vesicle membrane recycling.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f38/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Common locus to effector gene evidence

    A genetic association identifies a region of interest. Connecting it to a candidate variant, gene and tissue-specific process requires several kinds of evidence. Each step has uncertainty; proximity alone does not establish causality.

    Original URL kept: /science/process-atlas/f39/

    Read in shellOpen original page

  • Amphetamine dexamfetamine at the dopamine terminal

    Amphetamine mechanisms: experimental targets and clinical effects are distinct. A therapeutic dose or individual benefit cannot be read from this cartoon.
