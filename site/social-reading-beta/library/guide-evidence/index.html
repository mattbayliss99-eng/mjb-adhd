Guidebook pages on biology, evidence and treatment.

  • ADHD and biology — the overview

    ADHD is supported by converging genetic, developmental, brain and treatment evidence. There is no single cause and no diagnostic brain or gene test — but the core machinery is well understood.

  • Brain development and structure

    On average, the brain in ADHD matures on a later timetable, especially in prefrontal regions, with small differences in some deeper structures — largest in childhood.

    Original URL kept: /guide/static/evidence/brain-development/

  • The dopamine and noradrenaline supply chain

    Six steps — make, store, release, clear, break down, self-tune. Simple parts; the complexity is in regulation, timing and how the steps interact.

    Original URL kept: /guide/static/evidence/dopamine-system/

  • Genetics — many small influences

    ADHD is highly heritable, but through thousands of common variants of tiny effect plus rarer variants in some families. Genes describe risk in populations, not a diagnosis in a person.

    Original URL kept: /guide/static/evidence/genetics/

    Different studies point to different parts of the system, and some findings depend on medication history. ADHD is better described as several routes to a similar set of difficulties.

  • What treatment trials show

    Randomised trials show the medicines reduce core symptoms in adults; a withdrawal trial shows frequent symptom relapse in previously stable adult lisdexamfetamine responders after switching to placebo. Registries suggest wider outcomes may improve, with important caveats.
