What needs distinguishing

"It's genetic" gets heard as "one faulty gene". The real finding is closer to "many small dials and a few large switches". Keeping those two pictures apart is the whole point of this series.

Source passage: /science/genetics/#what-needs-distinguishing

No single "ADHD gene"

There is no single "ADHD gene". The picture is thousands of common variants that each nudge risk a tiny amount, plus rarer variants with bigger effects, plus chance and environment (Tier B).

Genes describe risk. They are not a diagnostic test and they do not tell a clinician which medication to use (Tier B).

Source passage: /science/genetics/#choose-a-genetics-topic

ADHD runs in families

ADHD runs in families. Twin studies put heritability at about 74% (Tier B).

Population findings describe groups; they do not predict one person.

Source passage: /science/genetics/#choose-a-genetics-topic

Two studies, two counts

Demontis and colleagues (2023) reported 27 genome-wide significant regions from ADHD diagnosis data

van der Laan and colleagues (2025) combined childhood symptom scores with diagnosis data and reported 39, of which 17 were new

The designs differ, so the counts are not a running total, and neither is a personal score.
