How evidence is labelled

Status: drafted · Content checked 2026-10-06

Eight labels describe what kind of claim each sentence is. Confidence and source access are tracked separately.

1 · Quick understanding

Every important sentence carries a label. Tap it to see the sources. A page can mix labels — a patient observation next to a research finding next to a hypothesis — and each keeps its own status.

  • Clinical guidance: What a national guideline, regulator or product licence says. Guidance shapes practice; it does not guarantee a particular prescription.
  • Research finding: A finding from published research. Check the study design: a trial, a registry comparison and a case report carry very different weight.
  • Institutional position: What an NHS body or service has said or decided, in its own records. It shows their position, not that the position is correct.
  • Patient observation: What a patient observed or reported. Recorded by a clinician or not, it remains a report until measured or independently corroborated.
  • Hypothesis: A proposed explanation that has not been tested. Included because it generates useful questions — never as a basis for changing treatment alone.
  • Contested account: Two or more accounts of the same event disagree. Both are shown; neither is treated as settled until records resolve it.
  • Missing source: A source that should exist and has not been obtained or checked. Absence from reviewed records does not mean the event did not happen.
  • Formal finding: A formal finding by a body with authority to make it — a coroner, ombudsman, regulator or court.

2 · Three separate questions

  1. What kind of claim is it? — the label.
  2. How strong is the evidence? — confidence: strong, moderate, limited or pending.
  3. What did we actually read? — access: full text, abstract, supplied document, or pointer only. A strong study read only in abstract is still marked as abstract.

3 · How to use it

  • Turn labels off with the Labels button for a clean read; turn them on to check.
  • Use the depth dial to stop at the level you need.
  • Hover any internal link for a preview; the map beside each page shows what connects to it.

5 · Release states

Pages move through: proposed → drafted → sourced → medically/editorially reviewed → integrated → checked → published. The current state is shown under each title.

Uncertainty

Labels are editorial judgements and can be wrong; the corrections log records changes.
