Burning, electric and touch-sensitive pain

Status: sourced · Content checked 2026-10-06

Pain quality helps describe the problem, but does not diagnose nerve damage. This page helps you connect sensations with their distribution, associated findings and assessment.

1 · Quick understanding

Peripheral nerve problems can cause burning or sharp pain, tingling, numbness and pain from light touch. Different sensory functions may be affected differently.

N4

2 · Possible explanations

Neuropathic pain means pain caused by disease or injury of the somatosensory nervous system. Assessment considers the history, a plausible anatomical distribution, examination findings and relevant investigations.

N5

3 · Useful next action

Mark affected areas on a simple body outline. Record whether contact, position or activity changes symptoms, and whether there is weakness or loss of sensation. Ask for a neurological and pain assessment.

4 · Treatment logic

Ask what the pain treatment is intended to improve and how benefit, sleepiness and everyday function will be reviewed. A cause investigation and symptom-relief plan are separate questions worth answering.

5 · Deeper explanation

Pain intensity and precise localization do not measure electrical charge, dopamine levels or progressive nerve injury. Proposed explanations should remain labelled as hypotheses until supported.

6 · Evidence and onward routes

Clinical information: N4N5. These are institutional explainers; the page is sourced, not independently medically reviewed.

For abrupt new neurological symptoms, use urgent medical assessment rather than this reading route.

Useful questions

  • Does the pattern fit a nerve, nerve root or another mechanism?
  • Which findings support that interpretation and what remains uncertain?

Uncertainty

Sensations can overlap between nerve pain and other pain mechanisms. This page provides a description route, not a diagnosis.
