A field guide to the nervous system

Status: sourced-draft · Content checked 2026-10-06

Start with the affected function, the clinical pattern and the decision you need. Follow the evidence beyond slogans about a single chemical.

1 · Quick understanding

The nervous system is easier to navigate through functions: starting an action, coordinating movement, sensing position, processing touch and pain, retaining an intention, and regulating sleep or autonomic activity. A symptom is an entrance into assessment; it is not a diagnosis.

2 · What a condition entry should explain

  • What the condition means, and which part of the nervous system is generally implicated.
  • What pattern or examination supports it, and what else can look similar.
  • What a useful simplified explanation captures, and where it stops being reliable.
  • What options target: symptoms, function, cause, or disease progression.
  • What is established, preliminary, disputed, or unknown.

3 · Choose an entrance

  • Parkinson disease: beyond the dopamine shorthand, Dopamine-responsive dystonia: a distinct clinical question, Dystonia: patterned twisting and spasms, Stiffness, spasms and spasticity, Coordination and balance: understanding ataxia, Hereditary spastic paraplegia: what assessment looks for.
  • Burning, electric and touch-sensitive pain, Strong sensation, unclear position feedback, Fatigue, sleepiness and sleep disruption, Memory, time and awareness.
  • Dopamine-related treatments: different jobs, Saffron and Mucuna: what the evidence actually says, My case: who owned the next decision?.

4 · Compare interventions fairly

Compare the actual problem treated, studied population, preparation, outcome, tolerability and access. A theoretical mechanism is one part of that comparison. An immediately noticeable effect is not by itself proof of long-term benefit.

5 · Audit a familiar answer

Ask: Which claim? Which source and date? Which population? What outcome? Which missing comparison? What would change this conclusion?

For a worked example, Saffron and Mucuna: what the evidence actually says compares small trials with broad public-information wording, and distinguishes studied preparations from shop products.

6 · Evidence and next routes

This page is an editorial navigation framework, not a systematic review. Condition explanations and pharmacological claims live on the linked sourced pages. Next expansion queue: peripheral neuropathy subtypes, functional neurological disorder, multiple sclerosis, migraine, autonomic dysfunction, epilepsy and sleep disorders. These are visible gaps, not completed coverage.

Uncertainty

This is the first field-guide section. It cannot yet claim comprehensive coverage of neurology. Some diagnosis terms describe syndromes, while others identify particular diseases.
