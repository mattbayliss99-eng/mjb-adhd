When a formulation feels different

Status: sourced · Content checked 2026-10-06

Record product differences precisely. A repeatable difference is worth raising even when the explanation is unknown — but observation and mechanism are different claims.

1 · Quick understanding

Some people respond differently to two products containing the same active medicine. That observation is worth recording precisely. The explanation is a separate question: product, strength, dose timing, food, other medicines, sleep and expectation can all play a part.

Matthew reports much better function on Amfexa 10 mg and 20 mg tablets than on earlier generic dexamfetamine 5 mg tablets, which he says he rarely collected because they did little.

J

He suspects a tablet ingredient (povidone or a related compound) reduces his response. No test has isolated that ingredient; it remains a hypothesis — see Open research questions.

2 · Possible explanations

ExplanationHow you might tell
Different dose actually takenCompare dispensing records and dose logs
Different release or absorptionTiming of onset and wearing-off, recorded the same way each day
Ingredient differenceSame strength, same timing, two products, consistent difference over repeated use — then discuss with a pharmacist
ExpectationEffects reported within seconds or minutes of swallowing are too fast for absorption
Other variablesFood, sleep, pain, other medicines on the same days

3 · Useful next action

Log: product name, manufacturer, strength, dose, time taken, food, other medicines, time of first noticeable effect, time it wore off, benefit, unwanted effects. Ask a pharmacist to confirm the exact product and its ingredient list, and ask your prescriber whether specifying the same product each time is clinically appropriate.

Do not test it by altering your medicine

Do not crush, dissolve, split or otherwise prepare tablets differently, or change doses, to test an explanation. That changes how the drug is released and can be unsafe.

4 · Treatment logic

Amfexa is not an "isomalt-only" tablet: the 10 mg tablet lists isomalt, magnesium stearate and yellow iron oxide; the 20 mg tablet lists isomalt, magnesium stearate and red iron oxide; and the 5 mg tablet also contains crospovidone.

W4

Specifying a product by brand is ordinary practice where there is a reason for it. Some NHS shared-care agreements already advise prescribing dexamfetamine by brand for ADHD.

One integrated care board's adult ADHD shared-care agreement states that only the Amfexa brand is licensed to treat ADHD and that generic 5 mg dexamfetamine tablets are not, and advises against prescribing generically.

P1

That is another area's document, not a rule everywhere — ask what your local agreement says.

Uncertainty

A product difference in one person does not identify its cause. Formulation science for these tablets in adults is not well studied.

Unresolved sources

The manufacturer of the generic 5 mg tablets previously dispensed has not been identified; dispensing history would show it.
