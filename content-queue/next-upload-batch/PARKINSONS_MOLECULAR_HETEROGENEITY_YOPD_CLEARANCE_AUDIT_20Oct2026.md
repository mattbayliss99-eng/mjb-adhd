# Parkinson's is not synonymous with low dopamine: molecular heterogeneity and the differential diagnosis

**Purpose:** Evidence audit for extending the EXISTING MJB Parkinson's field guide. Do not launch a duplicate page or infer a diagnosis. Patient-authored personal experience, medication trials and correspondence remain private and are **not** reproduced here.

## Proposed accessible explanation

A parkinsonian movement pattern and a particular underlying disease are not the same thing. Clinical Parkinson's disease is diagnosed through characteristic motor findings and contextual criteria; its familiar nigrostriatal dopamine dysfunction is only part of the biology. Distinct conditions involving alpha-synuclein dosage/aggregation, lysosomal processing, mitochondrial maintenance, dopamine synthesis, receptor/signalling dynamics or other pathological processes can converge on slowness, rigidity or dystonia while differing in non-motor burden, progression, biomarker signature and treatment response.

**Shared downstream phenotype ≠ shared primary etiology.** Dopamine replacement often improves symptoms but does not prove that dopamine deficiency was the initiating lesion, or that the treatment has altered the underlying pathological process. Conversely, a good levodopa response is evidence to evaluate, not a stand-alone Parkinson's diagnosis. The appropriate investigation is phenotype-first AND mechanism-aware: document positive motor examination findings, timeline, asymmetry, fluctuations, family history, autonomic/cognitive/ocular features, treatment response and adverse effects, then select discriminating tests or specialist referral.

## Evidence by level

| Claim | Evidence level, direct observation and limits |
|---|---|
| Clinical PD ≠ generic dopamine deficiency | Established clinical criteria: MDS 2015 define *parkinsonism* by bradykinesia plus resting tremor or rigidity, and use exclusions, red flags, supportive features to distinguish PD. These are operational diagnostic criteria, not a direct assay of disease pathogenesis. [MDS criteria PMID 26474316](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26474316/) |
| New classifications acknowledge molecular heterogeneity | Two **proposed research**, not clinical-service, schemes published in Lancet Neurology 2024: SynNeurGe separates pathological alpha-synuclein, neurodegeneration, genetic causes plus clinical component; neuronal-alpha-synuclein-disease integrated staging (NSD-ISS) uses pathological n-alpha-synuclein and dopaminergic function. They are not currently interchangeable with routine diagnostic criteria, and neither covers every parkinsonian mimic. [SynNeurGe PMID 38267191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267191/) ; [NSD-ISS PMID 38267190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38267190/) |
| Pathological alpha-synuclein differs among genetic groups | 2023 PPMI multicentre cross-sectional CSF seed amplification: 545 clinically diagnosed PD participants; overall sensitivity 87.7%; sporadic 93.3%, GBA variants 95.9%, LRRK2 variants 67.5%, healthy-control specificity 96.3%. Supports observable biological heterogeneity and limits of universal biomarkers; not a direct cause-of-progression experiment. Counts/phenotypes affect comparisons. [Siderowf et al 2023, full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10627170/) |
| SNCA gene dosage can cause neurodegenerative disease | Foundational familial studies link **SNCA locus triplication** and, separately, **duplication** to PD syndromes. They establish gene-dose causality in rare kindreds, not a claim that all PD is due to SNCA overexpression or that normal clearance is fully functional in those individuals. [Singleton et al 2003 PMID 14593171](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14593171/) ; [Ibáñez et al 2004 PMID 15451225](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15451225/) |
| Alpha-synuclein aggregates in SPG7 | **One published SPG7 p.Ala510Val homozygous autopsy** showed alpha-synuclein-containing Lewy bodies, tau pathology, neuronal losses and neuritic paraplegin/neurofilament accumulation. **The patient had no clinical parkinsonism**; authors explicitly left it unresolved whether alpha-synuclein/tau pathology was coincidental or SPG7-related. This is an unusually valuable *histological observation*, but NOT proof of a reproducible SPG7→alpha-synuclein causal route or universal SPG7 synucleinopathy. Earlier single SPG7 autopsy lacked alpha-synuclein/tau aggregates. [Thal et al 2015, PMID 26506339, open full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4632789/) |
| Early-onset or atypical levodopa response demands differential thinking | Dopa-responsive dystonia (including GCH1/BH4, TH and other synthetic/cofactor defects) can mimic parkinsonism, with variable levodopa responses and diurnal features; young age, dystonia or atypical examination may warrant tailored movement-disorders/genetic review. This does not make all YOPD misdiagnosed DRD. [Wijemanne & Jankovic 2015 PMID 26100751](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26100751/) ; [Salles et al 2021 PMID 34992971](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34992971/) |
| FND is not a default label for variable symptoms, nor eliminated by a drug response | FND requires *positive* clinical signs of internal inconsistency/incongruence, and can coexist with neurological disease. Presence of variability, distractibility, ADHD or nonspecific imaging does not independently establish FND. A levodopa response does not independently refute FND or establish PD. Ask for documented examination findings and interpretation. [BMJ Neurology Open 2025](https://neurologyopen.bmj.com/content/7/1/e000970) ; [2023 diagnostic pitfalls review PMID 37775197](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37775197/) |
| Choice about dopamine agonists is clinically meaningful | NICE NG71 §1.3.5 advises **offer levodopa** in early PD where motor symptoms impact quality of life. If symptoms do not yet affect quality of life, consider discussing levodopa, non-ergot dopamine agonists or MAO-B inhibitors. NICE §1.3.8 and §1.4 require counselling about adverse effects (impulse control, somnolence, psychosis); unwanted or poorly tolerated treatment merits specialist review, not an assertion that agonists are always inappropriate or advice to abruptly stop. [NICE NG71](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng71/chapter/Recommendations) |
| Mucuna ≠ co-careldopa; green-tea catechins ≠ validated carbidopa substitute | Mucuna contains actual levodopa; clinical trials generally small, defined product/sample, and outcomes often short-term. 2015 two-patient crossover found markedly lower L-DOPA exposure from Mucuna lacking pharmaceutical peripheral DDC inhibitor; 2017 randomised 18-person crossover assessed controlled seed preparations and differing levodopa exposures, not retail supplement interchangeability. **In vitro**, EGCG/EGC inhibited mammalian DOPA decarboxylase in a 2001 enzyme study; EGCG also inhibited COMT in human liver cytosol experiments in 2010. There is NO validated clinical evidence that a green-tea supplement provides reliably *peripheral-selective* DDC inhibition or predictable L-DOPA exposure analogous to pharmaceutical carbidopa. This combination should not be promoted or trialled as a DIY co-careldopa regimen. [2015 comparison PMID 26366963](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26366963/) ; [2017 RCT PMID 28679598](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28679598/) ; [DDC enzyme inhibition 2001 PMID 11374875](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11374875/) ; [EGCG/COMT laboratory study 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2916818/) |
| Green-tea EXTRACT and brewed tea have very different risk profiles | EFSA found food supplement intakes at or above 800 mg EGCG/day showed signals of liver injury; **it could not establish a universally safe extract dose**, and product-related liver injury has been documented below that value. LiverTox describes serious, occasionally fulminant liver injury attributed to concentrated green tea extracts. This cannot be translated into a recommendation to combine supplements with prescription dopaminergics. [EFSA 2018](https://www.efsa.europa.eu/en/press/news/180418) ; [NIH LiverTox](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK547925/) |

## Mechanistic model to explore (conditional, not proven in every PD syndrome)

**Distinct upstream perturbations:** SNCA gene dosage / fibril seeding; GBA1-lysosomal changes; LRRK2-associated intracellular traffic; PINK1/PRKN mitochondrial quality control; SPG7 m-AAA mitochondrial proteostasis/calcium handling; TH/BH4 precursor synthesis; AADC conversion.

**Shared/partly shared downstream disruptions:** proteostasis and vesicle cycling; mitochondria and energy availability; calcium/ROS buffering and ATP-dependent maintenance; synaptic dopamine availability; firing and motor-network output. Feedback cycles can amplify changes, but similar outputs are NOT statistically independent confirmation of the same upstream cause.

**Do not conflate:** (a) alpha-synuclein Lewy-body pathology with all protein aggregates, (b) protein aggregation with proof that deposited fibrils are the primary cytotoxic species, (c) dopamine depletion with PD diagnosis, (d) parkinsonism with idiopathic PD, (e) mitochondrial protease dysfunction with a specifically demonstrated global autophagy blockade, or (f) clinical levodopa benefit with confirmation of a specific genetic disease.

## Questions for a documented young-onset differential

1. What are the positive neurological findings: bradykinesia with decrement, resting tremor, rigidity, fixed versus variable dystonia, objective pyramidal signs, eye movement abnormalities, cerebellar signs? Which were absent, and by which exam?
2. If dopaminergic treatment helped, what exactly changed, with what objective baseline, timing, washout, reproducibility and measurement of autonomic/mood/attention confounding? Does the response distinguish PD from DRD or other conditions?
3. Which patient-appropriate tests actually discriminate the competing hypotheses: movement-disorder specialist assessment, targeted imaging where indicated, genetic counselling/testing, and selected metabolic/cofactor or neurochemical tests where clinically justified?
