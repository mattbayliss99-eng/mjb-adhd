# Motor-signal gain, timing and inhibition: why a neuron is not a passive wire

**Editorial gate:** General science, not a diagnosis, treatment recommendation, patient case report, or live website update. No unverified pharmacological combinations or specific patient histories in public repository. Extend existing pages, do not create duplicates. The evidence below distinguishes experiments, review synthesis, potential models and contradictory results.

## 1. Distinguish the output dimensions

For each treatment or proposed perturbation measure separately:

1. **Selection/initiation:** ability to trigger a specified movement, latency from cue to EMG onset.
2. **Recruitment/force:** motor-unit population and discharge rate, maximal voluntary contraction.
3. **Gain:** slope from measured incoming drive or stimulus to motor output, not inferred from mood/energy.
4. **Persistence and release:** delay to relaxation and return to baseline EMG, after-discharge, inability to release antagonist.
5. **Coordination:** agonist-antagonist co-contraction, sequencing, compensatory axial patterns.
6. **Feedback quality:** position/kinesthetic sense, muscle spindle Ia/II and Golgi Ib feedback, ocular/vestibular/plantar inputs.
7. **Autonomic safety:** standing/heat-associated pallor, sweating, nausea or presyncope require physiological evaluation; do not redefine as psychiatric/sensory by default.

Treat voltage-gated sodium and calcium currents, synaptic release, skeletal-muscle calcium handling and blood ion concentrations as **different compartments**. A supplement's general molecular affinity does not establish a therapeutically useful effect on the spinal motor circuit.

## 2. Canonical circuit map

Cortical and brainstem descending motor/postural drives and basal ganglia/cerebellar selection & prediction → spinal premotor interneuron networks (reciprocal, recurrent, presynaptic inhibition, reflex and patterned locomotor circuits) → alpha motoneurons (recruitment + rate coding + dendritic persistent inward currents/PICs) → motor units and muscle contraction, with continuous spindle Ia/II, tendon Ib, cutaneous, vestibular and visual feedback. Gamma motor neurons alter intrafusal muscle-spindle sensitivity.

**UMN vs LMN:** UMN is the descending CNS motor-control pathway, LMN the spinal/brainstem final motor-neuron output to muscle; neither is defined by literal distance. UMN dysfunction classically yields hyperreflexia, altered tone, spasticity and possible weakness; LMN dysfunction often yields reduced reflexes, weakness, reduced tone, atrophy/fasciculations. Sensory neuropathy and disorders of sensory integration require distinct branches. Source [E7].

## 3. Persistent inward currents (PICs), concrete candidate for gain and timing

Voltage-dependent persistent Na+ and L-type Ca2+ currents in spinal motoneuron dendrites amplify and prolong synaptic drive. Descending serotonergic (raphe) and noradrenergic (locus coeruleus) signals can increase PIC amplitude through relevant receptor signalling (e.g. 5-HT2 and alpha-1 mechanisms). PIC activity may promote force economy and self-sustained posture but excessively prolonged or poorly gated firing can impede precise transitions. Reciprocal inhibitory inputs can suppress activated PICs. The published review describes amplification up to fivefold in particular preparations: **not a quantification of clinical effect**. Source [E1,E2].

**Candidate failure patterns (not diagnosis):** (a) gain too low, insufficient automatic recruitment; (b) gain too high, postural overshoot; (c) too much persistence, impaired release; (d) poor reciprocal inhibition, co-contraction; (e) sensory estimate wrong, compensatory movement; (f) muscle length/contracture with no continued EMG activity. Distinguish in examination and relevant instrumental testing; symptom narratives alone are not enough.

**Spasticity vs dystonia discriminator:** a small (6 dystonia, 7 spasticity, 8 controls, age 6–25) EMG proof-of-concept found spastic patients activated multiple muscles with rapid passive stretch, while dystonic patients did so with voluntary movement [E6]. This is a small non-generalizable comparison, but supports studying actual activation patterns rather than labelling everything “stiffness.” Tests must be clinically supervised if there is pain, risk of falling or substantial impairment.

## 4. Serotonergic modulation is not synonymous with reducing drive

- A double-blind ~3-week escitalopram study in 66 healthy volunteers observed reduced **modelled reinforcement sensitivity**, not a general slowing of response time or universal improvement/deterioration [E3].
- A distinct double-blind eight-week trial in 128 healthy volunteers compared escitalopram with agomelatine (melatonin-receptor agonism and 5-HT2C antagonism). The January 2026 **unreviewed medRxiv preprint** reported improved task accuracy and less decision noise across active groups at eight weeks; earlier responses differed by drug [E4].
- Together these prohibit a fixed “higher serotonin adds one processing buffer” rule. Receptor subtype, cell, time, baseline state and measurable outcome must be specified. SSRIs treat depression/anxiety for some populations; subjective emotional blunting is clinically relevant but not a universal drug outcome.
- Noradrenergic and serotonergic descending spinal facilitation means these neurotransmitters may **increase** certain motor-neuron currents even where they blunt reinforcement in other circuits. No linear whole-brain claim.

## 5. Lavender, CBD, baclofen, magnesium: distinct levers, distinct evidence

| Exposure | Target / molecular-level finding | Clinical endpoint / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Silexan** (standardised oral lavender preparation) | 2013 experiments observed reduced voltage-dependent Ca2+ channel activity (including N- and P/Q-type) in selected neuronal systems; early screen reported no significant affinity for SERT, NET, DAT or MAO-A. Other studies implicate 5-HT1A-related changes. [E5] | Formulation-specific anxiety RCTs exist; cannot generalise to generic essential oil, directly predict PIC amplitude, prove effects on involuntary posture or instruct swallowing fragrance oils. |
| **Baclofen** | GABA-B agonism increases postsynaptic K+ conductance and inhibits presynaptic Ca2+-dependent release, often reducing reflex drive [E8]. | Symptom relief may occur without improving useful voluntary movement; sedation, weakness, fall risk and dangerous withdrawal or interaction risks. |
| **CBD** | Several candidate cellular targets incl. TRPV1, GPR55, voltage-dependent Ca2+ and Na+ currents; mechanisms vary by model/exposure. [E9] | Licensed CBD has indication-specific data in selected epilepsies; this does **not** validate a generic “turn off motor signals” combination or unsupervised dose escalation. CNS depression, drug interactions and impaired balance possible. |
| **Magnesium** | Essential ion and physiological NMDA channel block, but oral magnesium is not a selective CNS ion-channel drug. | Correct confirmed deficits when indicated; 2020 Cochrane muscle-cramp review found insufficient useful routine effect. Excess can cause toxicity, especially impaired renal handling. [E10] |

**No stacking protocol.** The goal of a research pathway is measuring restoration of correct motor output and function, not pushing neural signalling toward zero. In patients with abnormal tone, low tone can make transfers and balance worse. NICE NG220 explicitly warns that some people rely on spastic tone for standing and cautions about additive CNS depression with baclofen/gabapentin [E11].

## 6. Other botanical/nutrient critique and limits

- **Saffron:** mood trials can support a formulation-specific symptomatic hypothesis, but this does not demonstrate a clinically useful dopaminergic target or superiority over SSRI; assess clinical scales, study duration and heterogeneity in existing botanical article.
- **Ashwagandha:** stress/endocrine effects are under study, but claims of reduced menstrual pain through improved perfusion, restoration of dopamine supply or general haemodynamic 'harmonisation' lack adequate direct clinical proof. Safety issues include thyroid/liver reactions and pregnancy risk.
- **Tyrosine** is a catecholamine substrate, **creatine** a phosphagen energy-buffer constituent and **magnesium** an ion/cofactor, not interchangeable mechanisms or proof that more supply automatically increases functional neuronal output.
- **Omega-3** supports normal nutrition; ADHD symptom replacement efficacy is weak in reviews. Overstatements both for and against *all* mood effects should be corrected with indication-specific trials.
- **Food textures/preferences:** pattern recognition can guide careful assessment of pain, garment construction, taste/smell profiles, oral-motor function and nutritional adequacy; a single aversion neither confirms nor excludes autism, and food preference profiles alone do not establish a biochemical lesion.

## 7. Prioritised experiments/clinical assessment questions

1. **Is delayed relaxation active or passive?** Record targeted agonist/antagonist surface EMG at rest, ordinary task and recovery under professional supervision if clinically indicated. Compare the degree and duration of continued neural drive with passive joint restriction. Separate spasticity, dystonia, contracture and muscle relaxation deficits.
2. **Does postural failure reflect control, sensory estimate or autonomic physiology?** Evaluate strength, reflexes, tone, sensory position testing, vestibular examination, standing/lying blood pressure and HR, gait/transfer function. Do not provoke presyncope. Obtain relevant specialist examination and review for progressive abnormalities.
3. **Do medication-related functional differences dissociate attention from posture?** Prospective observation can describe the split across initiation, antagonist release, sustained alignment and objective function, but an observed response cannot identify DAT vs NET vs VMAT2 or diagnose a particular gene defect. Do not recommend cocaine or intentional challenge experiments.
