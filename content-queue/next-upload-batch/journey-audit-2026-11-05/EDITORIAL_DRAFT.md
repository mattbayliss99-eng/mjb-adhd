# My journey to restoring treatment: a patient-led audit

**Status: editorial draft for author review, 5 November 2026. Not approved for integration or publication on the live website.**
**Destination:** extend `/journey/`, with links from the existing accountability and treatment-continuity resources. Preserve the existing source register and earlier dated account.
**Scope of this first batch:** structure, opening narrative, and the funding/administrative sequence independently checked in correspondence. This is not the completed lifelong or ten-month audit.

## Proposed public opening

I had an established NHS ADHD diagnosis and approximately five years of specialist treatment. When continuity broke down, I spent roughly ten months trying to obtain an effective route back to treatment. I reported severe pain, mobility difficulties and loss of ordinary functioning while also coordinating referrals, records, complaints, funding requests and contact between organisations.

I ultimately identified, arranged and paid for a private transfer-of-care route through ADHD Direct. Treatment has restarted under that provider's supervision. Sustainable NHS funding remains unresolved.

This is my audit of that journey. Each entry separates what I requested, what the evidence shows happened, what the service says, what changed for me, and what remains disputed. It is a personal account supported by documents, not an independent clinical investigation or a court judgment.

Some NHS staff helped, issued referrals, supplied information or pursued escalation. Those contributions belong in the record. The central failure I am examining is that these activities did not restore an effective, sustainable treatment pathway before I secured the private route myself.

The question is not simply whether someone replied. It is whether the response produced an accepted next step, a responsible clinician, an accessible route and an actual decision.

## Reading the audit

A short account should remain readable without opening every document. Each event can expand to show:

- **Request:** the task, recipient and date.
- **Evidence:** a source identifier and publication-reviewed extract.
- **Response and outcome:** what was done, declined, deferred or left unresolved.
- **Service explanation:** its stated clinical, administrative or commissioning reason.
- **Impact:** distinguish contemporaneous patient reports from independently assessed harm.
- **Duty to examine:** the relevant rule, why it may apply, and what is still needed to determine a breach.
- **Status:** resolved, unresolved, disputed, or not yet verified.
- **Correction history:** retain a dated explanation when an entry changes.

“Breach established” is reserved for an identified authoritative finding. A complaint allegation and a professional standard must not be displayed as a finding.

## Rules and rights: reusable explanation panels

### Safe care and coordination

Regulation 12 of the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014 addresses risk assessment, reasonable mitigation and timely planning when care is shared or transferred. The audit should identify the known risk, the responsible provider, the action reasonably available and the evidence of what happened.

### Governance and records

Regulation 17 concerns effective governance and accurate, complete and contemporaneous records. An unanswered patient question is not by itself proof that no internal action occurred. Ask for the decision record, transmission record and acceptance evidence.

### Complaints

Regulation 16 requires an effective, accessible complaints process and necessary action on identified failures. CQC guidance says care must not be adversely affected because someone complains. A contact warning should be examined alongside the actual communication, staff-impact explanation, disability adjustments and preserved route to necessary care; the warning alone does not prove unlawful victimisation.

### Reasonable adjustments

Equality Act 2010 sections 20 and 29 are relevant to disability-related barriers in service access. Identify the disadvantage, requested adjustment, response and practical alternative. The duty does not mean every requested communication method must be used in every clinical circumstance.

### Professional continuity

GMC Good medical practice paragraph 65 addresses sharing information and communication needs, identifying responsibility and supporting continuity, particularly where patients struggle to navigate care. GMC standards are professional obligations; they are not themselves a court finding of negligence.

### What this audit cannot decide by itself

Clinical negligence requires assessment of duty, breach, causation and loss. A delayed application is not proof that earlier funding would have been approved. Neither medication response nor temporal association alone establishes the cause of all neurological symptoms.

Truth and public-interest defences exist under the Defamation Act 2013, but require their legal tests to be met. Publication should be accurate about both the underlying events and the overall implication conveyed. Names and GMC numbers identify professionals; they are not findings against them.

## Professional identification

Professional names may be included where necessary to explain a verified action or decision. Do not replace verified roles with anonymous euphemisms merely to avoid naming responsibility.

For every named clinician, check full name, role at the event date and identity against the official GMC register and relevant signed correspondence before adding a GMC number. **No GMC numbers have been verified in this first batch. Do not guess them.** Non-doctors do not receive GMC numbers.

Distinguish author, referrer, treating clinician, administrative sender, decision-maker and person merely copied. Do not assign responsibility simply because someone appears in an email.

Keep private contact details, signatures, unrelated third-party information and raw private clinical records out of the public repository. The public article uses source descriptions; publication-ready evidence extracts require separate review.
