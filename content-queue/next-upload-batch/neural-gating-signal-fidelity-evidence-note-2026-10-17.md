# Neural signal fidelity and inhibitory sensory gating

**Editorial status:** General scientific research note for staged editorial review, **not** a diagnosis, treatment suggestion or individual case account. Avoid inferring an individual's illicit substance exposure from the educational pharmacology discussion. This note must not be automatically deployed.

## Why a nerve can feel overactive without being usefully informative

Signal amplitude, frequency, timing, signal-to-noise ratio, recovery after activity and perceived salience are separate variables. Unpleasant tingling, burning or electrical sensations do not identify an anatomical location or a specific ion channel from sensation alone. An apparently quieter sensation can reflect less peripheral input, inhibition of transmission in the spinal cord, or a change in higher-level interpretation. These mechanisms cannot be differentiated through subjective relief alone.

## Evidence-supported inhibitory circuit

Descending noradrenergic pathways influence sensory processing in the spinal dorsal horn. Presynaptic α2 adrenergic receptors engage Gi/o-family signalling and inhibit voltage-gated calcium channel activity at relevant terminals, reducing release of excitatory transmitters. Postsynaptic α2-mediated potassium conductance can hyperpolarise some neurons. This is a location- and receptor-specific circuit, not a general property of sympathetic activation. Peripheral noradrenaline can sometimes increase nociception, especially in sensitised tissue.

GABA-B receptor signalling overlaps in its downstream effectors: presynaptic inhibition of voltage-gated calcium channels and postsynaptic G-protein-coupled inward rectifier potassium (GIRK) activity. Baclofen acts through GABA-B, not α2 adrenergic receptors. Sharing downstream inhibitory machinery does not mean two drugs have interchangeable effects or establish a lesion in the pathway.

**Evidence label:** Established circuit and receptor mechanism; any individual-case application is hypothesis only.

## Monoamine transporter operations are not voltage-gated sodium-channel operations

DAT/SLC6A3 and NET/SLC6A2 exploit ion gradients for transporter cycling. Amphetamine is a transported substrate with intracellular/vesicular effects and can produce transporter-mediated monoamine efflux. A cocaine-bound human DAT cryo-EM structure captured an outward-open inhibited transporter. These are distinct molecular operations, not simply two ways of increasing the same pool of transmitter.

Experimental work in DAT-expressing cells finds that intracellular calcium signalling, CaMKII and DAT phosphorylation can regulate amphetamine-dependent efflux. In one cell study, prior cocaine exposure blocked the amphetamine-associated intracellular calcium increase; this observation must not be translated into evidence of whole-brain or personal calcium correction.

Cocaine also blocks voltage-gated sodium channels through a separate local-anaesthetic property. State/use-dependent effects are demonstrated particularly clearly in cardiac NaV1.5 experiments and are a toxicity concern, **not evidence that an unmeasured systemic exposure selectively corrects ectopic peripheral firing**. A normal pulse does not exclude coronary vasospasm or conduction abnormalities.

**Evidence label:** Human transporter structures and primary cell/cardiac experiments established; therapeutic or symptom-specific extrapolation untested.

## Calcium is a cross-compartment variable, not a single defect

Presynaptic CaV channels regulate calcium entry and exocytotic transmitter release. Intracellular calcium can also influence monoamine-transporter regulation. Mitochondrial MCU/EMRE/MICU regulates matrix calcium import, buffering and metabolic responses. SPG7/AFG3L2 m-AAA protease activity supports balanced uniporter assembly by removing unassembled EMRE. These are physically and mechanistically distinct calcium-handling systems. Coupling them requires additional evidence for temporal and metabolic dependencies; an abnormal sensation is not an assay of any of these proteins.

Studies show different SPG7 or m-AAA perturbations can cause divergent calcium uptake outcomes; gene association alone must never determine a predicted direction.

## Candidate discriminators for a clinician/researcher, not personal drug experiments

| Candidate | What would separate it from alternatives |
|---|---|
| Peripheral ectopic firing / small-fibre disorder | Anatomical distribution; sensory and autonomic examination; targeted fibre investigations where appropriate; normal routine nerve-conduction studies do not exclude small-fibre disease |
| Spinal gating or central sensitisation | Stimulus-dependent pain amplification, sensory gain, clinician-assessed neurological pattern and relevant objective findings |
| Monoaminergic regulation of salience, motivation or descending control | Repeatable changes in specific functional domains under a stable prescribed regimen and controlled clinical assessment |
| Medication discontinuation | Timing of omissions and symptom emergence; known venlafaxine withdrawal includes paraesthesias, electric sensations and agitation |
| Mitochondrial maintenance deficit | Independently established genetic findings and laboratory/clinical phenotype; experimental mitochondrial assays rather than inferential symptom matching |
| Cardiac/autonomic effect | Clinical pulse, blood pressure, rhythm and ECG where indicated; watch pulse alone is insufficient |

The experimental priority is to separate **signal production**, **transmission**, **inhibitory gating**, **perceptual salience** and **energetic cost**, preserving adverse evidence. Avoid creating an unbounded causal chain from general biological plausibility to personal diagnosis.

## Clinical and safety boundaries

This article must not invite illicit drug experimentation, medication omissions or unsupervised supplementation as diagnostic tests. If someone has taken multiple stimulants, serious adverse effects are possible despite a comfortable subjective state and non-alarming pulse. Urgent chest symptoms, syncope, major neurologic changes, or cardiac rhythm symptoms require appropriate emergency assessment. Venlafaxine discontinuation can produce electric-shock/tingling sensations and agitation.
